Burton · Wachowiak · Castro — figure conventions

highconcmss Figure Style

For collaborators porting figures made in other software into the manuscript figure set. This guide is tool-agnostic — it states the target; how you hit it in MATLAB, R, Igor, Prism, or by hand is up to you. Where a rule exists for a non-obvious reason, the reason is given. Several are scars from Illustrator misrendering things, and skipping them costs an afternoon.

If you just need to make a figure

Go to for-collaborators/. It has a paste-anywhere prompt for any AI assistant, an importable Python module, and a Claude Code skill — all generated from one source, so they agree. This page is the reasoning behind what they do.

Prefer code to prose? style_reference.ipynb is this document as a runnable notebook — every rule demonstrated on synthetic data.

scopeWhat these rules were derived from

These conventions come from the analysis figures in figures/ — the NMF/PLS work, Fig 4 and supplements. The earlier drafts of Figs 1–3, 5 and 6 used a black-background hot colormap for ΔF. The standard sequential map is magma — black at zero, pale yellow at the ceiling, on the same dark ground (an earlier version of this guide set OrRd on white; the repo’s highconc.style.SEQ and the paper are magma now). One documented exception: raw photometric bulb activity maps keep their acquisition colormap.

00

The short version

If you do only five things:

  1. Export vector (PDF or SVG) with text kept as text — not outlines, not raster.
  2. Arial, 8–9 pt for tick labels and body text, 11–12 pt bold for panel letters.
  3. Sequential data → magma. Signed data → the teal↔red diverging map. Categories → the cluster colours. Never a rainbow, never jet.
  4. Top and right spines off. Ticks small or absent.
  5. Fix the colour scale across every panel that will be compared, and state the limits in the caption.
01

Colour

Sequential — activation and magnitudes

#000004 · lowmagmahigh · #fcfdbf

ΔF/F, magnitudes, densities, response matrices — anything running from “nothing” to “a lot” with no meaningful zero-crossing. Load seq_magma_256.csv if your tool doesn’t ship magma. In R it’s viridisLite::magma(256); Fiji ships the mpl-magma LUT. Figure 8’s p-value matrix is the one panel that keeps OrRd, passed explicitly.

Diverging — signed weights and differences

−max0 · gainsboro+max

PLS weight matrices, block structure, observed − predicted differences. The neutral midpoint is grey rather than white, so zero reads as “nothing here” and stays distinguishable from the page.

Rule

The midpoint must be zero. Set limits symmetrically (vmin = −vmax) or use a norm that pins the centre. A diverging map centred anywhere but zero is a bug, not a style choice.

Where a diverging map is needed but the quantity is non-negative — block norms, for instance — the same palette is reused from its midpoint upward. That’s intentional, for visual family resemblance across panels.

Categorical — the concentration conditions

The paper's primary categorical scheme, and the one in the most panels. Colourblind-safe, ordered weakest → strongest — which is the order they should appear in every legend. A condition is the same colour in every figure in the paper.

Rule

Nulls and shuffles are always grey, never a colour. The eye should read “this is the boring comparison” before it reads the axis label.

Greys, floors, and missing data

  • Zero is not missing, and they must not share a colour. A zero response is a measurement and belongs at the floor of the sequential ramp (black in magma). gainsboro #dcdcdc means not measured. On a sparse response matrix — and most of ours are sparse — colouring zeros grey turns the majority of the panel into “no data” and makes the figure claim something false. Keep vmin = 0 on sparse matrices and reserve grey for genuine gaps.
  • A floor above zero is a separate, deliberate choice. Example glomerular maps clip at vmin = 0.5, vmax = 3.5 in log(1+ΔF/F) so responders saturate and shrunken predictions become visible — a full-range scale hides that effect. But a floor hides data, so say in the caption that you set one and what it was.
  • Identity and unity lines: grey #737373, dashed, ~1.2 pt, drawn under the data.

What not to use

No jet, no rainbow, no hsv. No red/green as the only distinction between two conditions. Don’t recolour a panel to make it pop — panels sharing a quantity share a colour scale.

02

Typography

ElementSizeWeight
Tick labels8 pt (7 when crowded)normal
Axis labels, in-panel annotation9–10 ptnormal
Panel titles9–11 ptnormal
Panel letters (A, B, C…)11–12 ptbold
Figure-level titles12–13 ptbold

Font: Arial, with Helvetica and DejaVu Sans as fallbacks. Not because Arial is beautiful — because it is installed on the Macs where these get assembled, so Illustrator doesn’t flag imported text as a missing font and silently substitute it. If your tool defaults to something else (Helvetica Neue, Calibri, DejaVu), change it before exporting.

Sizes above are final, on-the-page sizes. If a figure will be reduced to fit a column, render it at the final size rather than shrinking it later.

Panel letters sit outside the axes, upper-left, roughly at axes-fraction (−0.10, 1.05). Capital, bold, no parentheses or periods: A, not (a).

03

Geometry and axes

  • Widths: single-column ~3.5 in, full-width ~7 in at final print size. Working renders in the repo are much larger (11–17 in) and scaled at assembly — if you’re handing over a finished panel, hand it over at final size.
  • DPI: 300 minimum for any raster; 600 for final submission. Working previews are 200.
  • Spines: top and right off on panels with data axes — scatters, lines, bars. Left and bottom stay. Matrices and images keep their full frame: there the frame is the extent of the data, not decoration, and dropping it leaves the cells floating.
  • Ticks: label size 8. On heatmaps, tick length 0 — labels only, no marks.
  • Grids: off, unless a grid carries real information.
  • Aspect: matrices whose axes are the same kind of thing (odorant × odorant, cluster × cluster) are square. Matrices whose axes differ (glomerulus × odorant) fill their cell.
  • Colorbars are shared. When several panels show the same quantity, draw one colorbar for the group, positioned just outside the block it serves. Per-panel colorbars imply per-panel scales.
04

By figure type

Scatter and parity plots

  • Dense clouds: small markers (area ≈ 1–7 pt²), no marker edge, alpha 0.2–0.6. Let overplotting show density.
  • Emphasis points: large (area ≈ 40–150 pt²), white edge ~1 pt, drawn on top. The white ring is what separates a highlighted point from the cloud.
  • Parity line: dashed, grey #737373, ~1.2 pt, underneath the data.
  • Parity panels are square, with equal x and y limits. Unequal axes misrepresent the deviation.
  • Put the statistic (r, Q², n) in the panel as text, not in the title — it survives cropping and reflows better in Illustrator.

Heatmaps and matrices

  • Nearest-neighbour interpolation, never bilinear. These are discrete measurements; smoothing invents structure.
  • Cluster and block boundaries: black, 0.8 pt, drawn over the cells.
  • Row and column order is meaningful — state the ordering (seriated? by cluster? by chemical class?) in the caption. Within a cluster, responders come before recruited/non-responders, contiguously.
  • Missing cells: opaque gainsboro — not transparent (see §6).
  • Small matrices (7×7, say) are drawn as vector rectangles, one per cell, not as an image (see §6).

Spatial maps — glomerular topography

  • Glomeruli as filled circles on a fixed spatial frame, magma, generous marker size. They should read as a map, not a scatter.
  • Fixed limits across every map in a comparison — low vs high concentration, observed vs predicted, subject vs subject. If two maps in one figure have different scales, the figure is making a false claim.
  • Sub-floor glomeruli in gainsboro, so they read as context.
  • One shared colorbar for the whole array.

Line plots and sweeps

  • Real series in colour; nulls and shuffles in grey.
  • Line weight ~1.5–1.8 pt; markers only when x-values are few and discrete.
  • Where a sweep has a chosen operating point (the selected NMF rank, say), mark it explicitly rather than leaving the reader to infer it.
  • Spread bands: filled, same hue as the line, low alpha, no edge.

Bar charts

Used sparingly in this figure set, deliberately — where a bar chart would show a handful of values, prefer a dot or strip plot with the individual observations visible. If a bar is right:

  • Zero baseline, always.
  • Single hue from the palette; don’t colour bars to differentiate them when the axis already labels them.
  • Show individual points over the bar when n is small.
  • No 3D bars, no gradients, no drop shadows.

Cumulative distributions

Preferred over histograms for comparing conditions: no bin-width choice to defend, and the whole distribution is visible at once.

  • One step line per condition in the condition colours, drawn weakest → strongest so legend order matches physical order. ~1.6 pt.
  • y from 0 to 1, labelled cumul. prob.
  • Where a specific quantile matters, mark it with a rule rather than leaving the reader to read it off.

Box plots

  • One box per condition, filled with the condition colour; black median and whiskers at ~0.8–1.0 pt.
  • Log axis where the quantity spans orders of magnitude — delivered concentration does. The “zero baseline always” rule is about bars; it does not apply on a log scale, where zero does not exist.
  • Show outliers as small diamonds rather than hiding them.

Rank-aligned response heatmaps

Each row sorted by magnitude and centred on its own peak, so tuning breadth is comparable across rows regardless of which odorant drove each one.

  • Sort descending, peak at the centre column, mirror out to ±N.
  • Label the axis +N | 1 | +N — it is a rank, not an odorant identity.
  • Pad short rows with NaN → grey, so a short row is visibly different from a row of zeros.

Category strips on matrix axes

  • One coloured segment per contiguous run, with the family name in the same colour.
  • Categories must be grouped in the plotting order — the strip makes that obvious, which is the point of drawing it.
  • Use one mapping across every panel showing the same categories.

Schematics

  • Same fonts and palette as the data panels, so a schematic doesn’t look imported from a different paper.
  • Large glyph letters (X, W, Y) at ~20 pt; captions at ~11 pt.
  • Build them so the pieces stay separable in Illustrator — grouped, labelled, not flattened.
  • 3D and WebGL scenes rasterise on export. Render those at high resolution as a static image and treat them as raster elements deliberately.
05

Export — the Illustrator contract

Every panel gets exported three ways:

FormatBackgroundPurpose
PNGwhitepreview, slides, emailing around
PDFtransparentassembly in Illustrator
SVGtransparentassembly, web

Non-negotiable properties of the vector exports:

  1. Text stays live text. Not outlines, not paths, not pixels. You must be able to click a label in Illustrator and retype it.
  2. Fonts embedded as TrueType (Type 42), not Type 3. Type 3 text arrives as unselectable garbage.
  3. Transparent background, so panels layer without white boxes.
  4. Cropped tight to the content — no acres of whitespace to trim by hand.

How to get there

ToolRecipe
matplotlibsvg.fonttype="none", pdf.fonttype=42, ps.fonttype=42; save with bbox_inches="tight" and transparent=True for pdf/svg.
MATLABexportgraphics(fig,'panel.pdf','ContentType','vector','BackgroundColor','none'). Avoid print -dpdf with painters on figures containing transparency. Check the result — MATLAB sometimes outlines text.
Rsvglite::svglite() keeps text as text (base svg() is less reliable); ggsave(..., device = cairo_pdf) embeds fonts properly.
PrismExport as PDF or EPS — not high-resolution TIFF. In export options, keep “Text as text”.
IgorExport as PDF with “Embed fonts” on.
If your tool can’t

If it simply cannot emit editable-text vector, say so and send the highest-resolution raster you can — 600 dpi at final size — plus the underlying numbers. A panel can be rebuilt from data, but not from a JPEG.

06

Illustrator gotchas

All of these are real failures that cost real time on this project.

6.1Missing-font substitution

Text exported in a font the assembling machine lacks gets flagged (pink highlight) and silently substituted, changing metrics and breaking layout. Arial avoids this. Check every imported panel for the missing-font warning before you trust it.

6.2Transparent rasters become soft masks

A raster with an alpha channel exports into PDF as a soft-masked image, and Illustrator renders those wrong. Make raster elements opaque. For empty or sub-threshold cells, set an opaque grey rather than leaving them transparent.

6.3Tiny rasters get dropped

A small image — a 7×7 heatmap, say — can be dropped entirely on import; the panel arrives with its cells simply missing. For any small matrix, draw each cell as an explicit vector rectangle. It looks identical in the PNG and survives the round trip.

6.4Never resize in Illustrator

Scaling a vector panel after import corrupts stroke weights and throws off font sizes relative to the rest of the page. Re-render at the target size instead, scaling figure dimensions, font sizes, line widths, and marker areas together. Marker area scales as the square of the linear factor.

6.5Use vector shading, not raster overlays

To wash out or highlight a region, draw a semi-transparent vector rectangle with a visible edge — it stays selectable and editable, and doesn’t trigger 6.2. A raster overlay does the opposite of both.

07

What gets hand-tuned

The repo’s stated principle is that outputs are reproducible from (config + data), and that touch-ups belong upstream in the generating code rather than on the exported file. In practice a small, bounded set of things are done by hand in Illustrator:

  • Multi-panel assembly — arranging panels into Fig 3, Fig 5, and so on.
  • Panel letters, placed at assembly time.
  • A few labels deliberately omitted from the scripts because hand placement beats any automatic rule — cluster labels on the concentration-flow figure are the example.
  • Callouts, arrows, and brackets that annotate rather than encode data.
Rule of thumb

If a change would alter what a reader concludes from the data, it must happen upstream and be reproducible. If it only affects arrangement and annotation, hand-work is fine.

Practical consequence: send the panel and the numbers behind it. A hand-edited .ai file with no path back to data can’t be regenerated when a reviewer asks for a change — and reviewers always ask.

08

Naming and provenance

Each figure lives in its own folder under a short descriptive name — not fig3, which changes as the paper is reordered, but pls_performance, nmf_per_subject, concentration_flow. Alongside the images:

  • caption.md — the caption text, version-controlled
  • source.md — where the data came from: paths, derivation, any SHAs
  • panel-level numbers as CSV where practical, with a column dictionary

When you hand a panel over, include the equivalent: what it shows, what produced it, and the numbers. That’s the part that makes a figure survive revision.

09

Known inconsistencies

Stated plainly so nobody ports a bug.

  • Font is not uniform. Nine of twelve generator scripts set Arial explicitly; nmf_clusters_main, pls_recruitment, and pls_result fall through to DejaVu Sans. (pls_result says it does so “to match the other figures,” which was true of an earlier state of the repo and isn’t now.) Arial is the standard; those three should be brought into line.
  • Working DPI varies — 120 to 300 across scripts. 300 is the floor for anything final.
  • Cluster-boundary colour varies — black in the scaffolded figure, blue at 50% alpha in some working panels. Black at 0.8 pt is the standard.
  • The older Fig 1–3/5/6 drafts used black-background hot (black → red → yellow) for ΔF heatmaps, ranked response maps and correlation matrices. They are on magma now, which sits on the same dark ground. Because zero is black, the zero/missing distinction above has to be made explicitly — missing cells in opaque gainsboro — where the old black ground hid both.
  • Raw bulb activity maps are a deliberate exception. The panoramic dorsal-surface overlays keep the acquisition colormap (a jet-like blue→green→red map) rather than magma. They are photometric images reproduced as acquired, not re-plotted data, and the field reads them that way. This is the only sanctioned rainbow map in the paper — do not generalise it to any panel you are plotting from numbers.
  • The diverging map is redefined in four separate files rather than imported from one place. It is identical in all four — but if you’re transcribing it, transcribe from the palette files here, which are the reference.
